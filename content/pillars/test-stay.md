## Quick start

- Aim for **two weeks minimum** if you want more than a holiday impression.
- Stay in **one main area**, not four. You’re testing rhythm, not collecting sunsets.
- Build at least **three normal days** into the trip: groceries, naps, school-run timing, bedtime, work calls.
- Tour **Empathy School** if learning is part of your plan.
- View housing only after you understand the area and the commute.
- Use this before you leave: [Test stay decision scorecard](/resources/test-stay-decision-scorecard).
- If you want the most relevant advice first, open [Family paths](/family-paths).

## What a good test stay actually does

A good test stay doesn’t prove that Bali is perfect.

It shows you whether a **specific version of Bali** works for your family.

That means:
- one or two realistic areas,
- a workable morning routine,
- a housing style that feels sustainable,
- a learning plan that doesn’t create chaos.

If you try to do everything in one trip, the signal gets blurry fast.

## The four things to test on purpose

### 1) Daily rhythm

Try at least a few ordinary days:
- wake-up,
- breakfast,
- one outing,
- one errand,
- bedtime.

This is the part families often skip. Then they make the biggest decisions using holiday energy.

### 2) Area fit

Area choice shapes almost everything else:
- noise,
- commute,
- community,
- walkability,
- how drained everyone feels by the afternoon.

Start here:
- [Areas](/areas)
- [Where to live in Bali for families](/guides/where-to-live-in-bali-for-families)
- [Ubud vs Sanur vs Canggu for families](/guides/ubud-vs-sanur-vs-canggu-for-families)

### 3) Housing reality

If the move feels plausible, start viewing homes on the same trip.
The safest sequence is usually:

1. short stay  
2. area shortlist  
3. in-person viewings  
4. verification  
5. deposit

### 4) Learning anchor

If you already suspect Empathy School could be the right fit, don’t leave the visit to the end.

Tour the campus. Notice the drive. Imagine a Monday morning.

## A simple two-week rhythm

You don’t need a minute-by-minute itinerary. You need structure.

### Days 1–3
Settle in, rest, get phones working, buy the basics, and let everyone decompress.

### Days 4–7
Live more normally. Test errands, transport, meals, nap timing, and one rainy-day plan.

### Days 8–10
Do the higher-stakes things:
- area comparison,
- housing viewings,
- Empathy School tour,
- one or two parent conversations if possible.

### Days 11–14
Stop adding things. Narrow the list.
By the end of the trip you want:
- one main area,
- one backup area,
- one clearer timing decision,
- one honest answer about whether the move is still appealing.

## What to write down before you fly home

Don’t trust the memory of an intense family trip.

Write down:
- where mornings felt easiest,
- what commute time felt acceptable,
- what kind of home felt good enough,
- what your children did when the trip stopped feeling novel,
- what made you feel calmer,
- what made you feel tight in the chest.

That last one matters more than people admit.

## What we learned the hard way

Families rarely regret doing a test stay.

They do regret asking the trip to answer every question at once.

The best trips narrow the field. They do not solve the whole move.

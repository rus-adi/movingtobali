## Quick start

- Fix **phones + internet** early. It solves more stress than people expect.
- Create one easy **food loop**: breakfast, snacks, groceries, water, dinner fallback.
- Choose your **default transport plan** for school runs, errands, and rainy afternoons.
- Know where your nearest **clinic** is before you need it.
- Keep the first month simple: one weekly anchor is enough.
- Use the [First month setup checklist](/resources/first-month-setup-checklist).

---

## The first 72 hours

Your job is not to become local overnight.

Your job is to reduce friction.

### Sort these first
- local SIM / phone setup,
- card or cash routine,
- water delivery / basic groceries,
- transport app or driver plan,
- one calm place the family can reset.

## The first week

This is when Bali starts to feel less like arrival and more like life.

### Build a baseline routine
You only need a few dependable pieces:
- breakfast that works,
- one grocery run you can repeat,
- one reliable transport option,
- one bedtime rhythm that survives tiredness.

## The first two weeks

Once the basics are working, you can start making better decisions.

This is usually the right time to:
- compare areas more honestly,
- do housing viewings,
- test a school commute,
- decide whether the move feels lighter or heavier than expected.

## The first month

The first month is mostly about energy management.

What often helps:
- one weekly learning or community anchor,
- a driver or route plan everyone understands,
- lower expectations for productivity,
- fewer “should we also…” decisions.

A calmer move usually grows from repetition, not constant optimisation.

## Health basics families forget until they need them

Know these before there is a fever, a fall, or a rough stomach bug:
- your nearest clinic,
- the hospital you would actually use,
- who calls the driver,
- where your insurance details live.

## The routine that changes everything

For many families, the move starts feeling real once there is one dependable weekly anchor.

That might be:
- school,
- camp,
- a recurring class,
- one regular family market morning,
- a standing playdate.

It does not need to be big. It just needs to repeat.

---

## What we learned the hard way

The move starts to feel easier once mornings and evenings stop being improvised.

That sounds obvious. It still catches families out.

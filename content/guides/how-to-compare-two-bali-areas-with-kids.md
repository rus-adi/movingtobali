## Quick take

Once the shortlist is down to two areas, stop asking which one sounds better.

Ask:
- which one protects mornings better,
- which one keeps the family calmer by late afternoon,
- which one makes Empathy School easier or harder if it matters,
- and which one still feels good when you imagine real groceries, rain, work calls, and pickup.

## Compare the week, not the map

The wrong comparison sounds like this:
- “This one feels more beautiful.”
- “This one seems trendier.”
- “This one looked calmer online.”

The better comparison sounds like this:
- Which one keeps mornings lighter?
- Which one makes groceries and ordinary errands easier?
- Which one still feels okay after a school run or a long afternoon?
- Which one supports the child we actually have?

## The five things worth comparing

### 1) Commute drag

Not only distance.

Compare:
- school-time traffic,
- late-afternoon return,
- how much buffer the route demands,
- whether the family would resent repeating it.

### 2) Family pace

Some areas support a slower, softer week.
Others support more convenience and activity.
Neither is automatically better.
But one is usually better for your family right now.

### 3) Child-stage fit

Toddlers, primary-age children, and teens do not want the same thing from an area.

Ask whether the area supports:
- short loops,
- child confidence,
- outdoor reset,
- or more independence and social energy.

### 4) Budget pressure

An area changes more than rent.
It can change driver dependence, convenience spending, and how much “easy” costs each month.

### 5) What housing becomes

A better area comparison usually makes housing simpler.
You stop searching all over Bali and start asking for the right type of help in the right radius.

## Where Empathy School changes the comparison

If Empathy School is part of the move, do not leave it until after you fall in love with an area.

A school question can change:
- which side of Bali deserves your time,
- how much commute friction is acceptable,
- what kind of house is even worth considering.

## A simple way to compare two areas in person

Try to repeat the same test in both places:
- one morning route,
- one grocery / essentials run,
- one tired-family late afternoon,
- one pass near the time you would be heading to or from Empathy School.

Then write one honest sentence per area:
- “This area protects the week by…”
- “This area quietly makes the week harder by…”

## The most common mistake

Families compare two areas like they are choosing between personalities.

What they are really choosing is the shape of the week.

That is why the best comparison is not the prettiest one.
It is the one that makes the rest of the move clearer.

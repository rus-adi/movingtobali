## Quick take

The first week is for **landing**, not impressing yourself.

The more tired your family is, the more your first week should help everyone feel safe, fed, shaded, and less confused.

## What to do first

### Day 1–2: Reduce friction

Your job is not to learn all of Bali.
Your job is to reduce the number of tiny problems.

Start with:
- water, snacks, and simple meals,
- cash / payment backup,
- SIM / internet setup,
- safe way to move around,
- bedtime that respects jet lag and exhaustion.

### Day 3–4: Learn your immediate loop

By this point, try to know:
- your easiest grocery option,
- one café or meal fallback,
- one pharmacy or clinic reference,
- what nearby traffic feels like,
- whether the family likes walking the area at all.

### Day 5–7: Test reality

Only once the family is more regulated should you add heavier tasks:
- view housing,
- test a school commute,
- visit [Empathy School](/schools),
- compare area feel by time of day,
- revisit budget expectations.

## What not to overload in week one

Try not to do all of this at once:
- long drives every day,
- too many social plans,
- too many house viewings,
- too much “we should make the most of this” energy,
- too many admin tasks stacked in one afternoon.

That kind of week can make Bali feel harder than it really is.

## A better first-week pattern

- **One real task per day**
- **One easy family outing**
- **One dependable meal plan**
- **One earlier bedtime than you think**

## When to bring in Empathy School

If school may be part of the move, week one can actually be a good time to test the idea.

Not because you must decide immediately.
But because it helps answer:
- would this make the week more stable?
- is the commute manageable?
- does it feel aligned with what we want for our child?

## Week-one warning signs

Pay attention if:
- every outing feels harder than expected,
- the area is beautiful but too draining,
- adults are starting to snap by midweek,
- the children feel constantly tired or dysregulated,
- the housing setup makes everything feel temporary in a bad way.

Those are not failures.
They are useful information.

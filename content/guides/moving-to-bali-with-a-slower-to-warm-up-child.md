## Quick take

A slower-to-warm-up child often needs a Bali move to begin through **trust**, not excitement.

That usually means:
- fewer firsts per day,
- repeated routes,
- more previewing,
- a home that feels regulating,
- and adults who are not interpreting caution as failure.

## What helps most

### 1) Repetition before expansion

Instead of trying to sample the whole island, repeat a smaller loop.

That might be:
- one café,
- one route,
- one activity,
- one school visit,
- one reliable home base.

Children who warm up slowly often give better signals on day three than on day one.

### 2) A calm home base

The right home matters even more when the child uses the house to reset.

Look for:
- sleep ease,
- shade or cool reset points,
- manageable noise,
- and enough predictability that the child can find their footing quickly.

### 3) Lower-pressure introductions

A school visit, camp, or activity may still help.
But the framing matters.

The day usually works better when it feels like:
- a first look,
- a gentle introduction,
- a way to gather signal,
- not a forced decision point.

### 4) Adults who protect the pace

A cautious child often needs the adults to slow the whole move down slightly.

That can mean:
- fewer appointments,
- fewer housing viewings in one day,
- less chatter about “loving Bali,”
- more note-taking about what is becoming familiar.

## Why a slower relocation path can help

Some families accidentally make the move harder by demanding clarity too early.

A slower path can look like:
1. short test stay,
2. review what felt safe versus too fast,
3. narrower area shortlist,
4. calmer second round of decisions.

## When Empathy School may still be a strong fit question

A slower-to-warm-up child can still respond beautifully to the right environment.

But do not ask a single school day to answer everything.

Use the visit to notice:
- whether the child seems able to settle with time,
- what parts of the environment feel regulating,
- whether the adults around children feel calm,
- and whether the family can imagine the day becoming familiar.

## What to test on a stay

Try to answer:
- What becomes easier by repetition?
- What stays hard even after a few tries?
- Which area makes the child calmer fastest?
- Does a more structured rhythm help?
- Is the family accidentally creating too many firsts at once?

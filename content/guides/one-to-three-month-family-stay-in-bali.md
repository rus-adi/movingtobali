## Quick take

A 1–3 month stay is not a holiday.
It is also not a full relocation.

The sweet spot is:
- enough structure to feel real,
- enough flexibility to change course,
- enough time for the first impression to wear off,
- not so much commitment that the stay becomes hard to read honestly.

Done well, this kind of stay can answer more than endless browsing ever will.

## What a short family stay is actually good for

A strong 1–3 month stay can help you test:
- whether Bali works for your family’s energy,
- which area feels livable,
- what your budget range really looks like,
- how your children respond after the first week,
- whether Empathy School should be part of a future move,
- whether you want to extend, pause, or go home satisfied.

## How not to turn it into a holiday bubble

If the whole stay is beaches, restaurants, and novelty, you will not learn much.

Build in:
- grocery runs,
- laundry,
- ordinary mornings,
- at least one rainy-day rhythm,
- a few repeated routes,
- one or two weekly anchors for the children.

## The best housing approach for 1–3 months

On a shorter stay, the goal is usually **good enough + flexible**.

That often means:
- not chasing the perfect forever villa,
- accepting a lighter setup,
- staying close to your priority area,
- keeping the process simple enough that you can still notice how life feels.

## Why structured time for kids matters so much

Short stays often work better when children have one or two anchors.

That might be:
- a camp,
- a weekly class,
- a repeated activity,
- a learning conversation or school visit if that is relevant.

The point is not to fill the calendar.
The point is to help Bali feel like a place you live in, not only pass through.

## Budgeting for a stay that teaches you something

Short stays can look deceptively expensive because the first month often carries:
- setup costs,
- transport learning curve,
- more convenience spending,
- “we do not know where anything is yet” money.

That is normal.
Use ranges instead of pretending month one is average.

## A simple rhythm that works well

### Month 1

Settle, learn the area, build the basic loop, and keep the schedule light.

### Month 2

If you are staying longer, start testing whether the setup still works without novelty.
That is when the useful truth usually appears.

### Month 3

Treat the last weeks as a review period:
- extend,
- shift areas,
- begin planning a longer move,
- or leave feeling informed instead of confused.

## The review questions that matter most

Before you extend anything, ask:
- Which part of Bali worked for our actual family?
- What felt easier than expected?
- What kept feeling heavy?
- Could we imagine a bigger commitment here?
- If Empathy School stayed in the picture, would the rest of the move still make sense?

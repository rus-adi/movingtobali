## Quick start

- Treat the visit as a **whole-week test**, not just a school tour.
- Start from a realistic base, not a fantasy villa.
- Pay attention to adult energy after the drive as much as the campus itself.
- If the day feels too stretched, believe that signal early.

---

## Why this matters

Families often visit a school and ask:

**“Do we like it?”**

That is part of the question.
But it is not the whole question.

The more useful version is:

**“What does liking this school do to the rest of our move?”**

Because once Empathy School feels real, the area shortlist changes.
And sometimes that is the most valuable thing you learn from the visit.

---

## Before the visit

### Pick two realistic area candidates

Do not test the commute from nowhere.

Pick the areas that are still genuinely alive for your family and imagine you already live there.

### Decide what kind of answer you need

For example:
- Does school make Ubud-side life feel more obvious?
- Does the commute feel lighter than expected?
- Does one area stop making sense the moment you picture drop-off and pickup?

That gives the visit a job.

---

## On the day, test three things

### 1) The route

Drive it like a parent.
Not like a sightseer.

Notice:
- how long it feels,
- what traffic does to everyone’s mood,
- whether the route feels repeatable,
- whether pickup would feel worse than drop-off.

### 2) The family’s nervous system

Sometimes the route is technically fine.
But the adults already feel depleted before the real day has even started.

That matters.

A move rarely fails because one spreadsheet cell was wrong.
It fails because the family’s nervous system is asked to do too much every weekday.

### 3) The after-school version of the day

Do not stop after campus.

Ask:
- Where would we go next?
- How would lunch work?
- Where would younger siblings nap?
- What would this day feel like in heavy rain?

This is often where a “beautiful idea” becomes either workable or not.

---

## What to listen for afterwards

Try to write one honest sentence.

Examples:
- “The school felt right and the route felt lighter than we expected.”
- “We liked the school but the weekly shape around it still feels too stretched.”
- “The visit made one area much more logical and one area much less logical.”

That sentence is gold.

It tells you whether school is clarifying the move or complicating it.

---

## When this saves families time

This approach is especially useful when:
- you are torn between beach-side convenience and Ubud-side rhythm,
- you are trying to protect energy in the first month,
- you do not want to sign housing before the school question is real.

---

## If the result is uncomfortable

Good.

That usually means the visit did its job.

Sometimes the useful result is not:
- “Yes, definitely.”

Sometimes it is:
- “Not from that area.”
- “Not until we simplify the plan.”
- “Only if we change what we think the move should look like.”

Those are still strong answers.

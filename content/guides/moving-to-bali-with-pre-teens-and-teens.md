## Quick take

With older kids, the move usually succeeds when they are not just being **brought along**.

They need to be able to imagine:
- where they would go,
- who they might meet,
- how they would move around,
- whether the home base feels good enough,
- what part of the week could actually be theirs.

Bali can work beautifully for this stage.
But it usually works because the family is honest about the tradeoffs.

## What matters most with pre-teens and teens

### 1) Social life is not optional

A good area for older kids usually supports connection:
- clubs,
- sports,
- creative communities,
- spaces where they can meet people consistently.

This is why older-kid area choice is not only about parents preferring a certain vibe.

Use: [Bali areas for teens](/guides/bali-areas-for-teens)

### 2) Independence has to feel realistic

Independence in Bali is different from independence in many home countries.

Ask:
- what will they be allowed to do on their own?
- how will they get there?
- what is actually safe and realistic here?
- will the home base make them feel connected or stranded?

### 3) The home still needs to be a recharge place

A teen-friendly move is not only about access and energy.
It is also about whether home feels restful enough after a busy day.

That means:
- enough privacy,
- enough quiet,
- enough room to reset,
- not spending the entire family budget on location alone.

Related: [Housing](/housing)

### 4) They need real voice

Older kids do better when they get to say:
- what they are excited about,
- what they are worried about,
- what would make the move easier,
- what would make it feel impossible.

You do not need to hand them the whole decision.
You do need to let them tell the truth.

## A strong move sequence for older kids

1. **Talk honestly before you book anything.**  
2. Do a **test stay** that includes ordinary days, not just fun ones.  
3. Visit areas that feel different from each other.  
4. If school matters, **visit Empathy School** on the same trip.  
5. Review the stay together before anyone starts forcing an answer.

## The questions worth asking on the stay

Ask your older child:
- Could you see yourself here after the novelty wears off?
- Did any area feel energizing without feeling chaotic?
- What would you need to feel less isolated?
- What kind of week could actually work for you here?
- What still feels like a hard no?

Try not to defend Bali immediately.
The useful answer is the honest one.

## When Empathy School fits into the conversation

If your family is considering Empathy School, make the visit concrete.

Do not ask, “Did you like it?” and stop there.
Ask:
- could you imagine a normal week there?
- what felt interesting?
- what felt unclear?
- what would make the commute feel manageable or too much?

Those answers are usually more useful than a simple yes or no.

## The most common mistake with older kids

Parents sometimes fall in love with the move before their older child can picture themselves inside it.

Then every practical question feels like resistance.

Usually it is not resistance.
It is information.

## Good signs the move might work

- Your child is becoming more specific, not just more emotional.
- One or two areas feel more plausible than the rest.
- You can picture a social plan, not just a location.
- Home, commute, and learning are starting to fit the same version of life.
- The family can talk about the tradeoffs without pretending they do not exist.

## Quick take

The useful question is not just:
**“Do we like Empathy School?”**

It is:
**“What does our whole week feel like if Empathy School becomes one of the anchors?”**

A strong tour helps you test:
- the commute,
- the morning energy,
- the pickup reality,
- and whether your housing shortlist still makes sense.

---

## Before the tour

### 1) Pick a real starting point

Do not test the commute from a fantasy villa.
Test it from where you are actually staying.

If you are on a test stay, that is enough.
The goal is honesty, not perfection.

### 2) Note your likely areas

Go into the tour with a short list.
Usually two or three areas.

If all of Bali is still in play, the tour will feel interesting but not decisive.

### 3) Bring two sets of notes

Bring:
- the [School tour question list](/resources/school-tour-question-list)
- the [Empathy School commute + routine test sheet](/resources/empathy-school-commute-routine-test-sheet)

One helps you ask better questions.
The other helps you judge the whole week.

---

## During the tour

### 1) Watch the energy, not just the answers

Listen to the answers, but also watch:
- how adults speak to children,
- how children move through space,
- where calm seems natural,
- whether you can picture your child settling there.

### 2) Notice the transitions

Transitions tell you more than polished explanations.

Look at:
- arrival flow,
- moving between spaces,
- how adults support children when energy changes,
- what the environment feels like once the novelty wears off.

### 3) Ask one question with your real life in mind

Not just:
- What do you offer?

Also:
- What kind of child usually settles well here?
- What do families find hard in the first month?
- Where do most families live, and how does that affect the school run?

---

## Test the commute like a parent, not a tourist

This is the part families skip too often.

If you can, do:
1. the drive in,
2. the visit,
3. the drive out,
4. and then one more normal family task afterwards.

That last part matters.

Can you still imagine:
- a grocery stop,
- work calls,
- a younger sibling’s nap,
- an afternoon activity,
- the general energy of the house later that day?

If the answer is no, the school may still be lovely.
But the week may not be.

---

## The question to answer after pickup

When the tour is over, write one honest sentence:

- **We can picture this week working.**
- **We like the school, but the routine feels too stretched.**
- **We need to change the housing shortlist if this school is part of the plan.**
- **We are not ready to use school as the anchor yet.**

That sentence is more valuable than twenty vague impressions.

---

## If Empathy School feels right

Then use that signal properly.

Go back and tighten:
- your area shortlist,
- your housing search,
- your commute expectations,
- and your budget.

## If Empathy School feels good but the week does not

That is still useful.

Sometimes the answer is not “no school.”
It is:
- different area,
- different housing,
- different timing,
- or more realistic expectations about the day.

This is why tours are most useful inside a test stay, not in isolation.

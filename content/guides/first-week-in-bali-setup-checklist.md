## Quick take

The first week does not need to be impressive. It needs to become **repeatable** fast enough that the family stops solving every basic thing from scratch.

## The first-week priorities

1. **Transport you understand**  
   Decide how you will do the next three school-like mornings before you do anything optional.

2. **A food loop that works when everyone is tired**  
   Find one grocery route, one breakfast default, one easy lunch, and one fallback dinner that does not create more thinking.

3. **Phone + internet stability**  
   Get your connection sorted early so maps, messages, work calls, and delivery apps are not fragile.

4. **One child anchor**  
   Even a tiny anchor matters: a tour, a camp day, the same park, the same café stop, the same evening ritual.

5. **A paper trail**  
   Keep photos or files for passports, entry documents, school notes, housing messages, and payment receipts in one place.

## A simple first-week sequence

### Day 1–2
- Land, rest, and keep the radius very small.
- Confirm transport basics.
- Set up water, snacks, and easy meals.
- Do not schedule the hardest decision on the first tired day.

### Day 3–4
- Run one ordinary morning.
- Pressure-test the commute or area route you are most likely to repeat.
- Buy the small things that make evenings calmer: chargers, rain cover, simple medicines, extra water bottles.

### Day 5–7
- Decide what is still unclear: area, school, housing, or budget.
- Use the rest of the stay or month to answer that question on purpose.
- Keep the family schedule lighter than your research brain wants.

## What families regret most

- trying to solve housing, school, visas, and community in the same forty-eight hours,  
- moving around too much,  
- letting adults stay in planning mode while children are already in adaptation mode.

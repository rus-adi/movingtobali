## Quick take

A good housing agent should make the process clearer.

Not pushier. Not foggier. Clearer.

## Before the first call

Ask:
- Which areas do you think fit our family and why?
- What lease lengths do you usually help with?
- What’s typically included in the homes you show?
- How do you handle maintenance or repair questions?
- What documents should we expect before paying anything?

You are listening for clarity, not sales language.

## During the viewing process

Ask:
- Who owns this property?
- Who signed the listing off?
- What is included in the rent?
- What is not included?
- If we like it, what happens next?

Then ask the same questions again later in writing.

If the answers move around, take that seriously.

## Before any money changes hands

Ask:
- What is the deposit for?
- When is it returned?
- What can it be deducted for?
- What is the payment schedule?
- Will we receive a receipt?
- Who signs the agreement?

## Questions about fit, not just paperwork

Families sometimes focus so hard on the contract that they skip the daily-life questions.

Ask:
- How noisy is this road at night?
- What do mornings feel like here?
- How far is the drive to Empathy School at real times?
- Where do families usually buy groceries nearby?
- What happens in rainy season?

Those answers matter just as much as the deposit clause.

## Red flags that should slow you down

- pressure to pay before you have documents,
- unclear ownership,
- “don’t worry about that” language,
- refusal to confirm details in writing,
- a price or story that changes mid-conversation,
- anything that relies on urgency more than clarity.

## What we learned the hard way

The best agents do not remove your responsibility.

They reduce the number of unknowns.

That is a much better standard.

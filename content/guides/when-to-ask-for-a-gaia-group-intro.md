## Quick take

A Gaia Group intro is usually worth making when the family can answer four questions with reasonable honesty:
- When are we moving or testing Bali?
- Which two or three areas are serious?
- What budget band is actually usable?
- What does the home need to do for our real week?

If those answers are still blurry, the better move is usually more planning first.

## The goal is not speed for its own sake

Families often feel pressure around housing because listings are emotional.
A beautiful villa can make the whole move feel urgent.

But the useful housing question is not,
**“Could we imagine living here?”**

It is,
**“Would this home still make sense once the route, the school question, and the ordinary week are real?”**

That is why [Gaia Group](/gaia-group) works best after the move has enough structure.

## Signs you are ready enough

### 1) Bali is down to a real shortlist

Not a mood board.
A shortlist.

If the family is still saying “maybe Ubud, maybe Canggu, maybe Sanur, maybe something else,” it is probably too early.

A good intro usually starts when the search has narrowed to two or three serious zones, often with help from:
- [Area Match](/area-match)
- [Compare areas](/compare-areas)
- [Commute reality](/commute-reality)

### 2) The budget has a comfort band and a ceiling

One hopeful number is rarely enough.
The more useful housing conversations include:
- the comfort range,
- the stretch ceiling,
- and what the family refuses to trade away.

If the numbers still move wildly from week to week, stop and use the [budget calculator](/budget-calculator) first.

### 3) The home brief reflects the week, not only aesthetics

The best housing briefs say something real about:
- naps,
- commute tolerance,
- work setup,
- noise,
- number of bedrooms,
- pool comfort or safety,
- and whether [Empathy School](/schools) is part of the move.

If the brief is still mostly photos and vibes, use the [housing brief builder](/housing-brief-builder) first.

### 4) You know whether school or commute could change the brief

This matters more than many families expect.

If Empathy School might anchor the move, or if the commute is already starting to shape which areas still work, bring that into the housing logic before asking for an intro.

## Signs it is too early

You probably need a little more planning first if:
- the family is still choosing between test stay and bigger move,
- the area conversation is still map-wide,
- the budget is still mostly wishful,
- or the home brief has not yet caught up with the real family week.

That is not failure.
It is often the exact pause that keeps the first partner conversation more useful.

## A calmer order that works well

1. Use [move timeline](/move-timeline) or [plan your move](/plan-your-move) if the whole move is still broad.  
2. Use [area tools](/area-match) and [budget tools](/budget-calculator) until the shortlist behaves like a real decision.  
3. Use [housing intro readiness](/housing-intro-readiness).  
4. Build the [family housing brief template](/resources/family-housing-brief-template).  
5. Then request the [housing intro](/contact?route=housing-intro&topic=Housing%20intro&partner=gaia-group-bali).  

## What we learned the hard way

Housing feels like the place where “the real move starts.”
Sometimes that is true.

But sometimes the real move starts earlier, when the family finally accepts that area, commute, school, and adult bandwidth need to shape the brief before a housing partner can really help.

## Quick start

- Get down to **two or three areas**.
- Choose them by **weekday life**, not by Bali fantasy.
- If Empathy School is part of the move, let that commute matter early.
- Only request housing help once the shortlist is real enough to guide it.

---

## What families usually do wrong

They compare areas as if they are choosing a favorite neighborhood for a holiday mood board.

That is not the job.

You are choosing the background for:
- waking children up,
- getting somewhere on time,
- finding food fast enough,
- surviving the hour when everyone is hot and slightly unreasonable.

That is why a good shortlist feels a little boring in the beginning.
It is built from friction, not fantasy.

---

## Start with the week you want

Before you compare any area, answer this:

### What do we want the week to feel like?

Usually the real answer is something like:
- calmer,
- easier,
- more practical,
- or more social / convenient.

Pick one first.

If you do not, every area starts sounding good because each one is solving a different problem.

---

## Then ask the four questions that actually narrow things down

### 1) What commute friction can we absorb?

Not the ideal commute.
The real one.

Can your family handle:
- a longer school run,
- more driving for errands,
- a house that is lovely but inconvenient by Thursday?

In Bali, this question quietly decides a lot.

### 2) Are we optimizing for calm or convenience?

These are not the same.

Some areas feel softer at home but create more driving.
Some feel more convenient but keep you inside a busier, more stimulated rhythm.

Neither is automatically better.
But pretending they are the same leads to bad choices.

### 3) Is Empathy School part of the move?

If yes, even as a maybe, stop treating school as a separate future decision.

School changes:
- which side of Bali feels realistic,
- what “too far” means,
- what kind of housing is still worth considering.

A family can like an area in theory and still dislike what it does to the school week.

### 4) What budget shape are we actually protecting?

Not just the monthly number.
The shape.

For example:
- Are you trying to avoid a premium housing band?
- Are you fine paying more for easier daily life?
- Are you trying to keep the first move light and reversible?

Areas quietly change the budget through:
- rent,
- transport,
- convenience spend,
- and how much you pay to remove friction.

---

## A good shortlist is usually made of contrast

A useful shortlist often includes:
- one area that feels like the **calmer bet**,
- one area that feels like the **easier / more convenient bet**,
- and sometimes one **stretch option** you are genuinely curious about.

That gives the test stay something real to compare.

If all three areas are basically the same category with different aesthetics, you are not really learning much.

## How to make the shortlist in 30 minutes

### Step 1
Use the [Area Match](/area-match) tool to get a starting set.

### Step 2
Open the top 4 area guides and delete the ones that clearly do not match your week.

### Step 3
Ask:
- Which area would still work if we were tired?
- Which area would still work if rain slowed everything down?
- Which area would still work if Empathy School became part of the plan?

### Step 4
Write down your final 2–3 areas and why each one is still alive.

That note matters. It stops you from being seduced by random listings later.

Usually after this.

## A better test-stay mindset

The goal of a shortlist is not to prove one area is perfect.

The goal is to notice:
- which problems disappear,
- which new problems appear,
- and which tradeoffs your family actually minds.

That is enough.

## Quick take

A single-parent Bali move usually works when the life you build is **more manageable than it looks**.

That means:
- fewer long drives,
- fewer “we can figure it out later” decisions,
- a home that is easier to run,
- a week with real anchors,
- and enough local support that the parent does not have to be a full logistics department alone.

## The biggest planning shift

Do not plan Bali like you are a two-adult family with extra flexibility.

Plan it like this instead:
- what shortens the daily loop,
- what reduces invisible load,
- what makes mornings less frantic,
- what happens if the child is sick or dysregulated,
- and what version of Bali still works on an ordinary depleted day.

## The four things that matter most

### 1) A smaller radius

Single-parent setups usually improve when the family stops crossing Bali for everything.

Look for:
- easier groceries,
- easier pharmacy / clinic access,
- short outings,
- fewer car hours,
- a route to school or activities that does not eat the day.

### 2) A house that is easy to carry

The right home is not always the most beautiful one.

It is often the one that makes daily management easier:
- less supervision friction,
- better airflow,
- simpler upkeep,
- easier rest,
- enough comfort without feeling oversized or impractical.

### 3) Child anchors that reduce the parent load

One or two strong anchors can change everything.

That might be:
- Empathy School,
- camp,
- a weekly activity,
- one repeatable social rhythm,
- or a first-month routine that removes constant daily inventing.

Related:

### 4) Backup plans before you need them

Single-parent families benefit from pre-deciding:
- sick-day support,
- transport fallback,
- where you go when the child is done for the day,
- and what part of the week must stay protected.

This is not pessimism.
It is how the move stays calm.

## When Empathy School can help

If Empathy School fits your child, it can do more than solve school.

It can also help with:
- rhythm,
- belonging,
- a steadier weekly structure,
- and making the day easier to picture.

But that only helps if:
- the commute is realistic,
- the drop-off / pickup rhythm works,
- and the rest of the week still has enough margin.

## A better move sequence for single parents

1. Start with a [test stay](/test-stay) if possible.
2. Narrow to the smallest useful area shortlist.
3. Build a simple budget around real convenience.
4. Decide whether school or camp would lighten the week.
5. Only then make heavier housing decisions.

## The review questions that matter most

- Does daily life get smaller and easier here, or just more beautiful from a distance?
- What happens when the child has a hard day?
- Could I carry this week alone without feeling permanently behind?
- If Empathy School or camp enters the week, does the day lighten or get more complicated?
- Is this setup kind to my future self?

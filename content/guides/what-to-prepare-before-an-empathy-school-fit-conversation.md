## Quick take

A good Empathy School fit conversation starts with one honest question:

**What are we hoping school will clarify for our family?**

It might be:
- whether school should anchor the move,
- whether a tour is worth planning during a test stay,
- whether the commute would still make sense,
- or whether the weekly rhythm around Empathy School would feel calm or stretched.

If you can name that, the conversation usually gets much better.

## You do not need a perfect plan

Families sometimes avoid contacting a school because they think they need everything decided first.
That is not true.

You do not need to know:
- the final house,
- the final visa,
- or the whole Bali plan.

You do need enough to make the school question real.
That usually means:
- children’s ages,
- your likely move or test-stay timing,
- the rough areas in the picture,
- and what kind of weekly rhythm you are trying to create.

## The most useful things to prepare

### 1) Your children’s ages and what tends to help them settle

A fit conversation gets stronger when it includes how your child actually tends to move through change.
You do not need a long biography.
A few real sentences are enough.

For example:
- needs routine quickly,
- warms up slowly,
- very social once settled,
- outdoor learning helps,
- long commutes usually drain them.

### 2) The likely move shape

Say whether you are:
- exploring,
- planning a [test stay](/test-stay),
- or preparing for a bigger move.

School questions sound very different in each stage.
The cleaner the move shape, the cleaner the answer.

### 3) The area shortlist, even if it is rough

School never sits outside geography.
If you are thinking about Ubud, Sanur, Pererenan, or another zone, say so.
That helps keep the conversation tied to commute and family rhythm instead of floating above them.

- [Area Match](/area-match)
- [Compare areas](/compare-areas)
- [Commute reality](/commute-reality)

### 4) The school question underneath the move

This is the part families often skip.

Try finishing one of these sentences:
- We want to know whether Empathy School should shape where we live.
- We want to know whether a tour would tell us something meaningful during a short stay.
- We want to know whether the weekly rhythm around school would feel calming or heavy.
- We want to understand whether school belongs early in the move, or later.

## When to tour, and when not to rush a tour

A tour can be helpful, but not every family needs one immediately.
A tour is usually more useful when:
- the family has protected enough time around it,
- the day still includes commute reality,
- and the adults are ready to notice what changes about the wider move if school becomes real.

## What not to do

Try not to ask for “general information” when the real question is narrower.
That often produces a polite answer but not a decision-making answer.

Also try not to ask school to solve every uncertainty in the move.
Sometimes the next step is still [general move planning](/contact?route=planning&topic=General%20move%20planning) or [areas + budget](/contact?route=areas-budget&topic=Area%20%2B%20budget%20question).

## The sentence worth writing down first

Before you contact, write this sentence:

**If Empathy School became part of our move, we think it would change:**

That sentence usually turns a vague inquiry into a useful one.

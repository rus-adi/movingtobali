## Quick take

Kerobokan is a connector area: not usually the dream, but often the place where families realise practical location can beat prettier branding.

## Who usually keeps Kerobokan on the shortlist

- Families who care about being between multiple useful zones.
- Parents who prioritise practical access over a strong area identity.
- Families who want a more grounded base while still reaching busier west-side options.

## What the family week tends to feel like

Kerobokan tends to feel like a working base rather than a destination. That can be helpful if your week needs efficiency and flexibility. It can feel underwhelming if you want your home base to carry a stronger lifestyle mood on its own.

## Why this area keeps working for some families

- It can make cross-area logistics easier.
- Families sometimes appreciate that it feels more functional and less scene-driven.
- Housing choices can feel more practical when you stop chasing a named hotspot.

## Tradeoffs to be honest about

- A connector area can still become tiring if every useful thing requires driving.
- The exact micro-area matters more than the label.
- It may not give the emotional “yes, this is Bali for us” signal some families want.

## How Empathy School changes the question

If Empathy School is important, Kerobokan is only worth pursuing if the commute tradeoff is fully intentional.

## Who probably should not start here

Families who want their home base to feel quiet, nature-led, or strongly community-shaped usually prefer a more distinct area.

## Best way to test this area

Use Kerobokan as a comparison case. If it makes the week easier without feeling too flat, it may deserve more respect than it gets online.

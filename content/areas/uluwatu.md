## Quick take

Uluwatu attracts families who want the south’s outdoor energy and scenery, but it only works well when the spread-out geography still feels worth it after repeated drives.

## Who usually keeps Uluwatu on the shortlist

- Families who love the south and want that landscape to shape daily life.
- Parents comfortable with a more spread-out radius.
- Families who prioritise outdoor lifestyle and home-base feel over quick convenience.

## What the family week tends to feel like

Uluwatu can feel expansive and energising, especially if your family genuinely uses the outdoor lifestyle it offers. The friction appears when the week demands more repetition, more transport, and more backup plans than the scenery alone can justify.

## Why this area keeps working for some families

- The area can feel spacious and visually relieving.
- Families who love outdoor movement may thrive here.
- It can create a strong “we live in Bali” feeling for the right household.

## Tradeoffs to be honest about

- The spread-out nature of the area can make ordinary life heavier.
- You need a real answer for transport, school, and tired evenings.
- What feels beautiful on arrival can feel far by the second week.

## How Empathy School changes the question

If Empathy School matters, Uluwatu is a deliberate tradeoff. Treat the school run as a decisive test, not a detail to figure out later.

## Who probably should not start here

Families who need a smaller radius, faster backups, or lower drive time usually shortlist it lower after a real test.

## Best way to test this area

Do one full day that includes transport at non-ideal times. That is usually where Uluwatu either becomes more real or falls away.
